# Supplementary experiments

Robustness and sensitivity analyses reported in the manuscript's Sections
3.1-3.4 and Supplementary Tables S15-S32, one folder per analysis. Each
analysis runs on the same underlying study data as the main pipelines (HMP2/iHMP
for IBD, curatedMetagenomicData v3 for obesity and geographic origin). See the
repo-root README's "Data" section for how to obtain that data; every script
reads `MBEC_DATA_ROOT` (see the repo-root README) to locate it.

| Folder | Question tested | Manuscript |
|---|---|---|
| `01_ibd_loso/` | Does the IBD comparison hold when each HMP2 clinical site is held out? | Section 3.3; Tables S15-S16; Fig. S1 |
| `02_geography_study_confound/` | How strongly are country and study identity confounded, and what survives a study-held-out split? | Sections 3.3-3.4; Tables S17-S18; Fig. S2 |
| `03_obesity_batch_signal/` | Does the obesity k-mer advantage persist within individual studies, where between-study signal cannot contribute? | Section 3.4; Table S19; Fig. S3 |
| `04_matched_dimension_check/` | Does the k-mer advantage persist when both representations use the same selection method and dimensionality? | Section 3.4; Tables S20, S23, S24; Fig. S4 |
| `05_hmp2_read_depth_sensitivity/` | Does read depth (50k/100k/200k reads per mate) change HMP2 k-mer performance? | Section 3.1; Table S21; Fig. S5 |
| `06_kmer_k_ablation_extended/` | How sensitive is performance to k in {3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10}? | Section 3.1; Table 1; Table S22; Fig. S6 |
| `07_complementarity_analysis/` | Do the two feature spaces capture non-redundant structure (unsupervised PCA)? | Section 3.6; Tables S26-S27; Fig. S7 |
| `08_corrected_resampled_ttest/` | Which of the 48 primary comparisons remain significant after accounting for overlapping training sets (Nadeau-Bengio corrected resampled t-test)? | Sections 2.7, 3.2; Table S32 |

## Main results

The manuscript is the authoritative statement of these results; summaries:

1. **IBD leave-one-site-out.** On the four sites where abundance and k-mer can
   be compared, abundance AUC was 0.52-0.59 versus 0.67-0.73 for k-mer and
   0.61-0.70 for fusion. A fifth site (Emory) had too few samples of one class
   for k-mer and fusion evaluation. With four sites and no formal test, this is
   a directional observation.
2. **Geographic origin.** Cramér's V = 0.91 between country and study; 10 of 30
   countries are represented by a single study. Under a study-held-out split,
   macro-AUC stays at 0.75-0.77 for abundance but macro-F1 falls to 0.05-0.08;
   a bounded k-mer pilot (n = 1,453) shows the same dissociation. These results
   characterize cohort structure, not transferable predictive utility.
3. **Obesity within-study test.** Under the mixed-study pool, k-mer leads
   abundance by 0.030-0.047 AUC. Within single studies the gap reverses for
   every classifier in three of six studies and for three of four classifiers
   in a fourth; it does not reverse in ZeeviD_2015 or NielsenHB_2014.
4. **Matched dimensionality.** At 250 matched dimensions in IBD, three of four
   classifiers retain a modest k-mer advantage (+0.019 to +0.030) and Random
   Forest favors abundance (-0.017); at 100 dimensions three of four favor
   abundance.
5. **HMP2 read depth.** AUC is essentially flat across 50k-200k reads per mate,
   within round-to-round SD. Only read count was varied; single- versus
   paired-mate extraction and first-reads versus random subsampling were not
   tested (see the manuscript's Limitations).
6. **k ablation.** k = 3 is worst for every classifier; k = 6-10 form a broad,
   non-monotonic plateau, with k = 8 highest in cross-classifier mean AUC by a
   small margin.
7. **Complementarity.** Top-5 PCs explain 63.5% (k-mer) versus 27.6%
   (abundance) of variance, and cross-representation PC correlations are weak
   (|r| <= 0.36).
8. **Corrected resampled t-test.** 10 of 48 comparisons remain significant
   (versus 21 of 48 by Wilcoxon), all for geographic origin: six favor k-mer
   (Random Forest AUC/AUPRC, all XGBoost metrics) and four favor abundance (all
   SVM-RBF metrics). No IBD or obesity comparison remains significant.

## Not included

- Self-supervised or transformer-based genomic representation baselines.
- Full-cohort (n = 22,397) k-mer re-extraction for the geographic study-held-out
  analysis; the k-mer analysis uses a bounded pilot pool.
